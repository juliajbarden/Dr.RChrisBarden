Overview

R. Christopher Barden, J.D., Ph.D., L.P. is a nationally recognized expert in psychology and law. He is a licensed psychologist (clinical and forensic), licensed attorney, research scientist, speaker, author, and national science-litigation-legislation consultant. Dr. Barden has published in the leading journals spanning child psychology, social psychology, clinical psychology, psychiatry, and law.

Dr. Barden has held university faculty positions in a highly ranked graduate science department, a highly ranked medical school, and a highly ranked law school. He has provided expert testimony in courtrooms across many states on psychological, scientific, and legal issues, and has delivered invited addresses to the American Psychological Association, the American Psychiatric Association, the American Bar Association, the U.S. Surgeon General's Conference, the International Association of Plastic and Reconstructive Surgeons, and numerous other professional bodies.

He has also provided training and consultation to state and federal investigators, including the F.B.I., Sheriffs' Office staff, and police investigators. Dr. Barden has served as a member of a State Higher Education Coordinating Board (MN), a member of a State Licensing Board of Psychology (MN), and as a State Special Assistant Attorney General (UT).

Legislative Work

With colleagues in law, medicine, and public policy, Dr. Barden drafted the Emergency Medical System for Children Act — model legislation mandating improvements in the emergency medical care system for children. This work was published in the Harvard Journal on Legislation. Some version of this legislation has been adopted in more than 20 states, representing a landmark contribution to children's healthcare policy in the United States.

Dr. Barden helped draft and enact successful national health care legislation, working across disciplines to achieve systemic improvements in medical and mental health systems for children and families.

Mental Health System Reform

Following his work on emergency medical systems, Dr. Barden undertook systematic efforts to reform the mental health system using a five-pronged approach of education, regulation, litigation, legislation, and prosecution. His focus was on eliminating dangerous pseudoscientific practices — including so-called "recovered memory therapy," "multiple personality disorder" diagnoses, and coercive "holding therapies."

Through a decade of successful civil litigation, legislative hearings, prosecutions, and licensing actions undertaken in collaboration with scientists, concerned families, mental health practitioners, and attorneys, these dangerous junk-science methods have been greatly reduced or eradicated across the United States.

Dr. Barden's current projects include reform efforts in the family law, mental health licensing, public education, and criminal justice systems.
